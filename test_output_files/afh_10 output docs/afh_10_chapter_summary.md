## Main Concepts
- Steep Turns: Steep Turns consist of single to multiple 360° and 720° turns using a bank angle between 45° and 60°, with the objective of developing pilot skill in flight control smoothness, coordination, and attention division. These maneuvers expose pilots to higher G-forces and require increased power to maintain altitude due to significant loss of the vertical component of lift.
- Steep Spiral: The steep spiral is a flight maneuver designed for rapidly dissipating substantial amounts of altitude while remaining over a selected spot. This maneuver involves a gliding turn of at least three 360° turns around a surface-based reference point, where the pilot maintains a constant radius by adjusting bank angle for wind drift.
- Chandelle: The Chandelle is a maximum performance, 180° climbing turn that begins from straight-and-level flight and concludes with the airplane in a wings-level, nose-high attitude just above stall speed. The maneuver's goal is to gain the most altitude possible for a given bank angle and power setting, with proficiency judged by the pilot's skill in maximizing climb performance.
- Lazy Eight: The lazy eight is a maneuver designed to develop proper coordination of flight controls across a wide range of airspeeds and attitudes, uniquely featuring constantly changing control pressures. This maneuver involves a symmetrical series of climbing and descending turns, where the airplane's longitudinal axis traces an eight on its side with segments above and below the horizon.
- Chapter Summary: This chapter summary explains that performance maneuvers are designed to develop a pilot's skills in coordinating flight controls, dividing attention, and applying flight fundamentals, integrating these into advanced maneuvers. Developing highly-honed skills in performance maneuvers allows pilots to progress toward the mastery of flight, where control application becomes subconscious.
