# Chapter Summary: Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3C) Chapter 1: Introduction to Flight Training Introduction

## Overview
Chapter 1 provides an overview of flight training, including the role of regulatory bodies, training sources, and safety practices. It covers 14 sections, focusing on the foundational aspects of becoming a competent pilot.

## Key Learning Objectives
- Understand the role and standards set by the FAA for aviation safety.
- Identify the sources and structures of flight training.
- Recognize the importance of safety considerations in flight training.
- Learn about the Airman Certification Standards (ACS) and Practical Test Standards (PTS).
- Appreciate the continuous nature of pilot education and the value of the FAA WINGS program.

## Main Concepts
- Role of the FAA: The FAA prescribes safety standards for civil aviation, including the certification of airmen and aircraft, through the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR).
- Flight Standards Service: The FAA's Flight Standards Service sets aviation standards for airmen and aircraft operations, with local Flight Standards District Offices (FSDOs) serving as the primary interface with the aviation community.
- Role of the Pilot Examiner: Pilot and flight instructor certificates are issued by the FAA upon completion of required tests, with certain responsibilities delegated to Designated Pilot Examiners (DPEs).
- Role of the Flight Instructor: Flight instructors bear full responsibility for pilot training, emphasizing airmanship, decision-making, and safety practices.
- Sources of Flight Training: Flight training sources include FAA-approved pilot schools, non-certificated flying schools, and independent flight instructors.
- Airman Certification Standards (ACS) and Practical Test Standards (PTS): Practical tests are administered using ACS and PTS, which contain the standards and policies for performing maneuvers and procedures.
- Safety Considerations: Basic flight safety practices and procedures should be emphasized from the first dual instruction flight.
- Collision Avoidance: Pilots must maintain vigilance at all times to avoid midair collisions, adhering to the "see and avoid" concept.
- Runway Incursion Avoidance: Vigilance during taxi operations is crucial for avoiding runway incursions, with clear communications and situational awareness being key.
- Stall Awareness: Understanding the causes of stalls and how to recover from them is critical for safe flight.
- Use of Checklists: Checklists are vital for ensuring that critical items necessary for safe operation are not overlooked.
- Continuing Education: Continuous learning and feedback are essential for maintaining safety and achieving success in aviation.
- FAA WINGS Program: The FAA WINGS program offers continuing education opportunities to enhance pilot proficiency and safety.

## Key Terms and Definitions
- **FAA**: The Federal Aviation Administration, responsible for regulating and overseeing all aspects of civil aviation in the U.S.
- **FSDO**: Flight Standards District Office, the local division of the FAA's Flight Standards Service.
- **DPE**: Designated Pilot Examiner, a private individual designated by the FAA to conduct practical tests for pilot certification.
- **ACS**: Airman Certification Standards, the FAA standards for pilot skills and knowledge.
- **PTS**: Practical Test Standards, the FAA standards for the performance of maneuvers and procedures during practical tests.

## Summary of Important Facts
- The FAA adopts an operational training concept that places full responsibility for pilot training on the flight instructor.
- FAA-approved pilot schools are allowed to meet certification experience requirements with less flight time due to their structured environment.
- The 'see and avoid' concept is fundamental to collision avoidance, requiring vigilance from pilots at all times.
- Stall awareness training is mandatory before a student pilot's first solo flight, emphasizing the importance of understanding the angle of attack.
- The use of checklists is a critical component of flight safety, ensuring that no critical steps are missed during flight preparation and operation.
